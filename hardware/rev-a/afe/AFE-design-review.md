# CuraLink Rev A AFE circuit proposal

**2026-10-01 · Candidate engineering implementation; not fabrication released.** This file supports the root schematic handoff. It does not accept or rewrite handbook decisions. Component topology and calculations are concrete; missing qualification results are identified separately.

## Functional objectives and decision criteria

Condition eight 47 kΩ neural-like sources and a shared reference into approximately 1.5 V centered, 300 Hz–7 kHz signals on a 3.0 V rail. Retain 1 mVpp input headroom, allocate no more than 6 µVrms electronics noise over 300 Hz–7 kHz, minimize battery current and PCB area, preserve input fault limiting, and expose intermediate nodes so the first PCB can be diagnosed. The ADC/comparator endpoint is a root-system choice; this candidate drives an ADC directly without passive output DC recentering.

The old ≥10 MΩ whole-input-network objective is **not met**. A revised loading criterion is recommended below; no requirement is silently changed here.

| Meaningful option | Description | Advantages | Disadvantages / result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed TLV9004 / TLV9054 | Use low-power amplifiers for input, high-pass/gain and low-Q filtering, and faster units for final high-Q filtering / ADC drive. | 4.32 mA typical amplifier allocation; small quad packages; calculated noise margin; robust driver tuning access. | More passive matching than an INA; ADC RC, finite-GBW accuracy and 3 V behavior require qualification. Recommended concrete circuit. |
| All TLV9004 | Keep the same functional topology with every unit using the lower-current family. | 2.16 mA typical amplifier allocation; common pinout. | Final-driver settling and capacitive loading have weaker margins. Retain as a later power-reduction experiment, not this ADC draft. |
| All TLV9054 | Use the faster family throughout. | Better bandwidth/noise margin and simple common stocking. | 11.88 mA typical amplifier allocation, with little demonstrated benefit in the early gain stages. Same RUC footprints permit a qualification-driven substitution. |
| INA333 front end | Use gain about 10, followed by necessary high-pass, additional gain and filtering. | Precision DC gain/CMRR; 50 µA typical per INA; attractive input-stage power. | G=100 has only 3.5 kHz bandwidth, so it cannot provide the required early gain. G=10 bandwidth is 35 kHz; input-plus-output noise is about 53.9 nV/√Hz, giving 4.41 µVrms over the rectangular signal band before protection/reference noise. A shared REF buffer avoids eight INA bias/capacitance loads but adds its own noise. More single-IC packages and tighter noise margin than the mixed quad design. |
| INA321 front end | Low-current CMOS INA with minimum gain 5. | Approximately 40 µA and useful bandwidth. | 100 nV/√Hz alone gives 8.19 µVrms over 300 Hz–7 kHz, above the electronics objective. Not recommended. |
| MCP6004 | Similar low-bandwidth general-purpose CMOS quad. | 1 MHz and about 28 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz; readily understood architecture. | 100 µA typical versus TLV9004's 60 µA; ±4.5 mV listed offset and 0.6 V/µs slew provide poorer driver/headroom margins. It is not pin compatible with RUC. Not recommended. |

## Exact functional circuit

The machine-readable source is [afe-pin-nets.json](afe-pin-nets.json), generated by [build_afe.py](build_afe.py). Every physical amplifier pin is enumerated; schematic units A–D share one BOM reference, and unit 5 contains supply pins.

**Input and shared REF:** connector → TPD4E05U06 ESD shunt → 5.1 kΩ → series 10 nF C0G → noninverting input. Each input has its own 22 MΩ return to VMID. Use Rg=1 kΩ to VMID and Rf=10 kΩ from output to the negative input, giving gain 11. All nine input paths have the same topology; REF_PRE drives eight low-impedance high-pass reference branches. No channel connects a 4.7 nF electrode shunt.

**Differential high-pass / gain:** CH_PRE → 10 nF → 51.1 kΩ → negative input, with **two 909 kΩ in series** as output feedback. REF_PRE → matched 10 nF → 51.1 kΩ → positive input, with another two 909 kΩ in series from positive input to VMID. Both branches are 1.818 MΩ. Signal polarity is REF minus CH. Ideally,

`V_HP − VMID = (1.818 MΩ / 51.1 kΩ) × sRC/(1+sRC) × (REF_PRE − CH_PRE)`.

This gives G=35.577 and fc=311.46 Hz. Exact resistor is RT0603BRE07909KL / [C3014193](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C3014193.html), 0603, 0.1%; the independently varying series pair is represented in the calculation. The 1% 10 nF C0G part is KEMET C0603C103F3GACTU / [C2167903](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C2167903.html). Every channel needs its own REF high-pass branch; a shared reference signal does not mean sharing all these capacitors/resistors. This deliberately changes gain by 1% from the initial 1.8 MΩ calculation to use a sourced precision part.

**Two Sallen-Key sections:** each has equal 22.6 kΩ series resistors, a 1 nF capacitor from the resistor junction to that section's output, and a 1 nF capacitor from its positive input to VMID. The feedback gain pairs are 10 kΩ / 1.5 kΩ for section 1, and 10 kΩ / 12.4 kΩ for section 2. Gains are 1.15 and 2.24, Q values about 0.5405 and 1.3158, and nominal pole frequency 7042.25 Hz. This approximates a fourth-order Butterworth response; it is not an exact polynomial after finite amplifier bandwidth and tolerances.

**Output:** the final section outputs are AFE_OUT_1 through AFE_OUT_8. The MCU sheet owns the ADC isolation resistor and reservoir capacitor. Initial 47 Ω / 1 nF values failed the nominal driver test; the recommendation is **150 Ω / 470 pF with about 1.656 µs acquisition**, supported by the stress results below. Do not add a series output recenter capacitor: the differential-stage capacitors already block early-stage DC errors, and a capacitor-coupled ADC needs a separately qualified charge-domain model.

**VMID:** U109B follows power-sheet VMID_RAW, feeds VMID through 47 Ω, and uses 4.7 µF from VMID to GND. Feedback connects to VMID_BUF, before the isolation resistor. Effective capacitance after voltage derating should remain at least 2 µF. U109C/D are stable unused followers of VMID_RAW. The model of shared VMID impedance and all eight channels remains a qualification gate, including crosstalk; the linear noise calculation uses ideal VMID.

## Calculated gain, noise, headroom and matching

[afe-calculations.json](afe-calculations.json) is generated by [calculate_afe.py](calculate_afe.py). The model uses a dominant-pole 1 MHz amplifier in early stages and 5 MHz in the final stage, typical noise fits, 300 K resistor noise, explicit common-mode/differential input capacitors, 47 kΩ CH and REF sources, and assumed 20 pF FFC plus 2 pF board trace per input. No manufacturer macro-model, clipping, ADC charge injection, leakage statistics or VMID noise is included in this linear calculation.

| Quantity | Calculated result |
|---|---|
| Nominal cascaded gain | 1008.118 V/V, 60.07 dB |
| Gain at 1 kHz | 968.76 V/V |
| Gain at 300 Hz / 7 kHz | Approximately 701 / 704 V/V |
| Electronics noise, 300 Hz–7 kHz | 3.44 µVrms when output noise is divided by 1 kHz gain; 3.51 µVrms when frequency referred |
| CH+REF source-resistor noise, same band | 3.17 µVrms / 3.23 µVrms respectively |
| Electronics + source quadrature sum | Approximately 4.68 µVrms using the 1 kHz-gain convention; ADC/interference excluded |
| Attenuation relative to 1 kHz gain | 27.31 dB at 15 kHz; 43.60 dB at 23 kHz; 69.39 dB at 43 kHz |
| 300 independent tolerance samples | 1 kHz gain 964.9–972.0 V/V; 7 kHz gain 664.9–739.2 V/V |
| Modeled 1 kHz passive-tolerance CMRR | Minimum 50.44 dB, median 62.39 dB |

At 1 kHz, 50/200/500/1000 µVpp inputs produce about 48/194/484/969 mVpp outputs. At nominal 1.5 V center, the 1 mVpp test stays near 1.02–1.98 V, allowing substantial DC/headroom margin. Synthetic pulses must still be checked for bandwidth-induced amplitude change and ringing; sinusoidal gain does not establish spike detection sensitivity.

Input-equivalent peak signal / modeled total RMS noise is about 5.2 for a 50 µVpp sinusoid and 2.1 for 20 µVpp; actual spike duration and filtering change that ratio. Thus the 20 µVpp stretch remains unproven, and 50 µVpp is a sensitivity characterization case rather than an unconditional count guarantee. A ±100 mV input step would initially command ±1.1 V at the gain-11 preamp, near the intended 0.4–2.6 V output region, but the downstream high-pass/gain stage can clip during that artifact and needs blanking/recovery qualification.

At **DC**, high-pass input capacitors are open and the difference stage's noise gain is **one**, not 36.577. Its offset therefore propagates through the two low-pass gains without multiplication by the signal gain. Using ±1.6 mV listed amplifier offset bounds gives a final ±11.83 mV sum from difference + LP1 + LP2, or ±14.78 mV using ±2 mV values. These limits are listed at 5 V; allocate ±25 mV and qualify at actual 3 V. Add bias-current differences through 1.818 MΩ, VMID error and board leakage. Early preamp offsets are blocked after settling. Bias current in TLV9004 is typical-only; it is not a guaranteed worst-case input-loading/offset limit.

At a 30 kS/s endpoint, the first input band folding into the 300 Hz–7 kHz output band begins at 23 kHz. A 1 mVpp input blocker there is about 6.61 µVpp input equivalent after filtering, comparable in scale to the modeled noise; a 100 µVpp blocker is about 0.661 µVpp. Specify allowed out-of-band interference and verify analog alias rejection; the 7 kHz corner alone does not close this requirement. At 50 kS/s, the first corresponding edge is 43 kHz with stronger attenuation, but root MCU timing/power must justify that rate.

CMRR numbers above exclude amplifier intrinsic mismatch and layout/source asymmetry. With equal 47 kΩ sources, just 2 pF extra CH capacitance gives 47.70 dB CMRR at 7 kHz, and 5 pF gives 39.74 dB. A 10 mV common-mode disturbance at 50 dB corresponds to approximately 31.6 µV differential input equivalent: precision resistor labels do not guarantee rejection of large cable interference. Match CH/REF routing and source resistance; measure common-mode rejection across the actual cable and populated board.

## Input-loading requirement reopening

For a 22 MΩ bias return, ≥10 MΩ magnitude at 7 kHz allows only about **2.03 pF total effective shunt capacitance**. The amplifier's 5 pF common-mode input capacitance already exceeds that; connector/trace/ESD/cable add more. Its differential input capacitance is partly bootstrapped: `Ceff = Ccm + Cdiff × (1 − Vminus/Vplus)`. The model explicitly connects the differential capacitor between inputs; it does not incorrectly add the full differential capacitance to ground. Bootstrapping does not remove common-mode or FFC capacitance.

With zero FFC capacitance and assumed 2 pF board trace + 0.5 pF ESD, modeled impedance is about **3.0 MΩ at 7 kHz**. With a 20 pF FFC it is 0.826 MΩ, yet loading is small because it is mainly reactive:

| Assumed FFC capacitance per conductor | 47 kΩ source: voltage loss / phase at 7 kHz | 100 kΩ source: voltage loss / phase at 7 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| 0 pF | 0.27% / −0.95° | 0.53% / −1.94° |
| 20 pF | 0.42% / −3.31° | 1.19% / −6.93° |
| 50 pF | 0.96% / −6.83° | 3.49% / −14.20° |
| 100 pF | 2.63% / −12.57° | 9.96% / −25.28° |

**Recommended replacement objective for review:** at 47 kΩ, input loading ≤1% amplitude loss and ≤5° added phase over 300 Hz–7 kHz; at 100 kΩ, ≤2% and ≤10°. These are functional waveform-error limits, not an accepted replacement requirement. A short FFC with measured effective input capacitance near or below 30 pF per conductor is consistent with these limits under the stated amplifier/board assumptions. If the ≥10 MΩ requirement is retained, a different front end and guarded/interconnected cable architecture are required.

Measure loading without probing the high-impedance node: keep REF quiet, measure AFE output gain and phase with the source branch resistance bypassed, then repeat with 47 kΩ / 100 kΩ in series using the same stimulus/cable. The response ratio cancels the downstream filter and reveals source-loading amplitude/phase. Ordinary 10× scope probes can add approximately the same order of capacitance as the target itself, so do not infer intrinsic input loading while that node is probed.

## Actual LTspice verification, 2026-10-02

The [full LTspice review](../ltspice/afe/AFE-LTspice-review.md) uses original-equation TI amplifier family models and validates all 36 amplifier operating points. It reports 969.744 V/V at 1 kHz, leakage-aware steady DC rejection (18.36 uV total ADC baseline change over +/-100 mV with declared 10 GOhm capacitor insulation), nominal common-mode response, offset-step saturation/shared-bias artifacts and cold startup. Startup requires 2.336 s for the quarter-ideal-LSB check; 2 s fails. The nominal family-model result resolves the prior interconnected ngspice adapter operating-point failure. Hardware/PVT, board leakage, ESD, noise, actual ADC and nonlinear power-transition qualification remain open.

## Protection, startup and first-board access

TPD4E05U06 is connector ESD protection with about 0.5 pF typical line capacitance and a 5.5 V operating range. It is not a precision 3 V input clamp. Put it at the connector with short return paths, before the 5.1 kΩ resistor; use the same placement on REF. Current limiting, AC blocking and the amplifier's rail diodes form distinct functions. A 10 V quasi-static clamp voltage and a 3.5 V positive input rail-clamp level would imply approximately 1.27 mA through 5.1 kΩ, below the amplifier's ±10 mA absolute input-current limit; this arithmetic is not an IEC board-level pulse proof because fast parasitics and pulse energy matter.

The 10 nF / 22 MΩ input return has τ≈0.22 s. A fixture DC bias different from VMID can initially clip the preamps and cause large downstream artifacts. The earlier 2 s startup proposal failed the nominal LTspice quarter-LSB settling check. Recommend a **Candidate 3 s blank after cold startup**, with whole-AFE rail/baseline/clipping validity before counts. After cable reconnection or a large offset event, blank the shared AFE until validity is established; the tested nominal offset sequence recovers within a quarter ideal LSB after 1.585 s, but no universal fixed delay is qualified. This is not a guaranteed recovery time for every fault. The ±100 mV DC robustness test is an input-offset/recovery qualification, not a claim that signals remain valid during the step.

AC coupling does not make an unpowered input safe from backpower: a changing fixture waveform can still inject current through input rail clamps while AVDD is off. Disable the fixture output during CHARGE/system-off, and test injected-current/AVDD rise with the actual LDO and isolation path. A simulated ideal zero-volt supply can absorb current and conceal this problem. Do not rely on MCU firmware to prevent rail injection from an already running external waveform source.

Place local 100 nF supply bypass at every amplifier IC and 4.7 µF per bank. Keep high-impedance input routes short, clean and away from BLE edges, the antenna feed and switching current loops; avoid unnecessary ground copper directly under those short sensitive nodes. Use a continuous common electrical ground with placement/return discipline. Leave output/HP/preamp/REF/VMID test pads, and provide source resistor bypass and individual input isolation on the fixture. A probe on an input node is a diagnostic load, not a transparent observation.

## Simulation evidence and outstanding release gates

Official [TLV9004](https://www.ti.com/product/TLV9004) and [TLV9054](https://www.ti.com/product/TLV9054) pages offer their TLV9002/TLV9052 family PSpice models. Original archives, libraries and hashes are under [vendor](vendor/sources.json). [build_vendor_decks.py](build_vendor_decks.py) produces an adapter and gain/spike/offset/startup/ADC decks. The adapter namespaces helper circuits, reorders resistor syntax, changes the noiseless-resistor temperature from 0 K to 0.01 K, and disables zero-mean diode noise generator ports for ngspice DC/AC/transient compatibility. It must not be used to claim vendor-model noise results.

**Historical ngspice adapter failure (superseded by valid nominal LTspice analysis):** ngspice's interconnected TLV9002-family operating point fails or settles to the wrong VMID in this compatibility adapter. An isolated TLV9002 follower diagnostic does converge to 1.500398 V at a 3 V supply; therefore the interconnected failure does not establish that the physical VMID buffer is defective. An exit code of zero is not a passing electrical result. The gain/noise/tolerance table comes from the separate explicit linear MNA calculation. Use original vendor libraries in ADS/PSpice or repair and independently validate ngspice compatibility before using nonlinear full-AFE results. Existing full-AFE CSV traces are retained as failed diagnostic data; do not use them as gain, spike, offset or startup results.

The standalone final TLV9052-family filter/driver reaches the expected DC point and was simulated with a 1 kΩ SAR sampling switch and 5/10 pF hold capacitors. Nominal ADC RC and acquisition sweeps are in [adc-sweep/results.json](adc-sweep/results.json); polarity/tolerance and 1/7 kHz periodic-track results are in [adc-stress/results.json](adc-stress/results.json). For **150 Ω / 470 pF / 1.656 µs**, both 2.8→0.2 V and 0.2→2.8 V, R±1%, C±5%, 5/10 pF sampling gave worst **24.26 µV / 0.0331 LSB**, versus a quarter-LSB target of 183.1 µV at Vref=3 V. Across a 1.5–1.8 µs acquisition window, worst residual was 107.18 µV / 0.1463 LSB, so the result is not only a nominal ringing zero crossing. The 150 Ω / 1 nF alternative also passed (38.32 µV at 1.656 µs; 43.06 µV across that window).

At periodic 30 kS/s acquisition with the prior hold precharged to 2.8 V, 1/7 kHz final-filter input sines of 0.15 V peak around VMID produced ≤4.09 µV extra settling error compared with a duplicate continuously tracked quiet-reference circuit. Comparing directly to the instantaneous quiet ADC pin instead includes the normal 1 kΩ × sample-capacitance track delay; the 7 kHz / 10 pF case reached 168.30 µV / 0.230 LSB for this waveform. This is not a proof for larger/faster input slopes. The simulation represents typical family-model behavior and a simplified SAR load; it does not qualify real ADC conversion kickback, op-amp process corners, quad-package coupling or actual-board noise.

Before manufacture: complete exact passive BOM and footprint audits; close the output RC/acquisition budget; reproduce circuit response with validated nonlinear models; verify VMID stability/crosstalk, noise/bias/leakage at 3 V, input loading with the actual cable, offset/startup/unpowered cases, BLE-induced artifacts and alias rejection. After assembly: measure gain/corners, electronics versus source noise, CMRR/source imbalance, rail validity and recovery, ADC adjacent-channel history and device current. The existing handbook requirements remain authoritative until the root task records explicit acceptance or reopening.

## 2026-10-01 addendum: 50 kS/s periodic driver qualification target

The root integration proposes **50 kS/s per channel** as the preferred qualification target, subject to MCU clock/timing/power review. This moves the first image folding into the 7 kHz band to 43 kHz, where the explicit linear AFE model gives 69.39 dB attenuation relative to 1 kHz. The 30 kS/s alternative begins at 23 kHz with 43.60 dB attenuation and remains a fallback requiring an explicit out-of-band blocker limit. No physical AFE nets or parts changed for this check.

[stress_adc_50k.py](stress_adc_50k.py) generates four 50 kS/s final-driver decks and [summarize_adc_50k.py](summarize_adc_50k.py) produces [adc-stress-50k/results.json](adc-stress-50k/results.json). The same 150 Ω / 470 pF interface, 1.656 µs acquisition, 1 kΩ sample switch, 5/10 pF hold capacitor and prior-channel recharge to 2.8 V are used. Each 2 ms simulation evaluates 85 samples after 300 µs, covering a full 1 kHz waveform and the repeating 7 kHz / 50 kS/s phase positions. The filter-input sine is 0.15 V peak around VMID; this is a driver stress stimulus, not a measured extracellular waveform.

| Sine frequency / sample capacitor | Extra settling error versus quiet tracked reference | Instantaneous error including normal SAR track delay |
|---|---|---|
| 1 kHz / 5 pF | 1.46 µV / 0.0020 LSB | 11.87 µV / 0.0162 LSB |
| 1 kHz / 10 pF | 3.55 µV / 0.0048 LSB | 24.26 µV / 0.0331 LSB |
| 7 kHz / 5 pF | 1.61 µV / 0.0022 LSB | 98.17 µV / 0.1340 LSB |
| 7 kHz / 10 pF | 4.30 µV / 0.0059 LSB | **196.83 µV / 0.2687 LSB** |

The extra settling contribution remains comfortably below a quarter LSB, as do the previously reported static polarity/passive-tolerance tests. **The total instantaneous 7 kHz / 10 pF result does not pass the quarter-LSB criterion** of 183.1 µV at Vref=3 V. Most of that error is the normal switch-capacitor tracking delay; it must not be relabeled as a passing total-error result. Dividing its 196.83 µV output error by the 1 kHz AFE gain gives approximately 0.203 µV input equivalent, or approximately 0.28 µV when frequency referred using the 7 kHz gain. Those values are small compared with the calculated electronics noise, but they do not qualify the real ADC's accuracy.

This supports the 50 kS/s driver candidate without an RC/net change. Real SAR conversion kickback, actual switch/capacitance values, amplifier process/temperature corners, quad-package coupling, clock availability, acquisition phase and assembled-board behavior remain verification gates. The interconnected full-AFE vendor-model compatibility issue remains separate and unresolved.
