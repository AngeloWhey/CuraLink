# Four-channel AFE and grounded-coax bench interface

**2026-11-22 · ADS 2026 Update 2 · simulation evidence for the benchtop board**

The four-channel population keeps CH1–4, shared REF and the shared VMID follower. The preferred generator fixture drives four grounded coaxial connectors through low source impedance and puts each 47 kΩ electrode-emulation resistor at the board end of its cable. The connector shells connect to board GND. A dedicated local REF path establishes the quiet electrode reference through the same nominal source resistance as a signal channel. The existing AC-coupling capacitors and 22 MΩ bias paths bring the internal op-amp inputs up to 1.5 V. No coax shield connects to VMID.

## What was simulated

Each four-channel run uses the project’s translated TI-family TLV9002/TLV9052 equations, the unchanged four populated channel circuits, 3 V AVDD, 27 °C and ideal lumped fixture passives. The schematic uses the quad family parts. ADS solved 20 amplifier DC points and four independent AC excitations at 96 frequencies from 100 Hz to 10 MHz. Only frequencies up to 7 kHz describe the proposed coax bench signal use. The higher frequencies address the VMID feedback loop. The assumed cable capacitance in the coax cases is 100 pF per cable. Measure the actual cable and attenuator Thevenin impedance on the bench.

| Four-channel case | Differential gain at 1 kHz (V/V) | Generator to board-input amplitude / phase at 7 kHz | Magnitude of load at 7 kHz | Generator to ADC gain at 7 kHz (V/V) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Final 910 kΩ native schematic, board-side 47 kΩ | 970.78 | 99.819% / −0.916° | 2.962 MΩ | 706.60 |
| Earlier 909 kΩ model, 50 Ω source → coax → board-side 47 kΩ → AFE | 969.70 | 99.819% / −0.916° | 2.962 MΩ | 705.85 |
| 50 Ω source → generator-side 47 kΩ → coax → AFE | 969.51 | 97.452% / −12.536° | 0.2112 MΩ | 689.11 |
| 50 Ω source → coax → AFE, emulation bypassed | 971.77 | 99.9998% / −0.014° | 0.2112 MΩ | 707.34 |

**Measurement plane.** The load column is voltage at the AFE input connector-equivalent node (historical net name `ffc_ch1`) divided by current into it from the source branch. In the board-end case the plane is downstream of 47 kΩ. The 100 pF cable remains upstream, so this number measures the modeled AFE load including 2 pF trace, 0.5 pF clamp and amplifier parasitics. In the generator-end and direct cases the cable shunts that same input node. The direct 50 Ω fixture delivers nearly the whole source voltage even though the cable loads the port. The ≥10 MΩ requirement at 7 kHz remains unmet even with the board-end emulator. At 7 kHz 10 MΩ would allow at most 2.27 pF for a purely capacitive shunt and less with a finite resistive shunt.

The REF fixture includes the matching 47 kΩ + 50 Ω to the quiet reference, or 50 Ω in direct-drive mode. It includes the same board-side 2 pF trace and 0.5 pF clamp assumptions. The physical REF sees no fifth 100 pF cable. In the diagnostic common-mode test ADS conceptually drives the input side of all five source equivalents together. With the high-resistance element at the generator end, the unequal CH/REF cable capacitance reduces modeled CMRR from 72.86 to 30.58 dB at 1 kHz. For actual common-mode injection provide a test contact or removable REF-to-ground link that allows driving REF through the chosen reference impedance. Do not connect a source against a hard ground.

**DC bias and input current.** The board-end configuration places all four ADC pins at 1.503153 V and VMID at 1.500441 V. All 20 amplifier outputs are at least 0.2 V from either rail. Their inputs lie inside the rails and differential DC input magnitudes stay below 1 mV. ADS gives 2.430915 mA from the ideal 3 V analog source, about 7.29 mW. The historical eight-channel simulation gave 4.471805 mA under corresponding conditions. The 2.041 mA reduction applies to this analog fixture. Radio, digital rail, charger, regulator quiescent current and total battery runtime require their own budgets.

**Steady DC rejection.** The [nine-point ADS offset sweep](coax-offset/dc.csv) uses the updated native 910 kΩ stage resistors, the 47 kΩ local reference and an assumed 10 GΩ leakage across each of the 13 DC-blocking 10 nF capacitors. Sweeping just CH1 from −100 to +100 mV moves the first preamp baseline 4.897 mV and the ADC1 baseline **18.525 µV** end to end (0.0253 ideal 12-bit LSB at 3 V). A step or disconnected electrode can still saturate the coupling stages during recovery. Measure recovery time before enabling spike counts.

## Nominal electronics noise

ADS's translator drops the vendor model’s noiseless-resistor parameter. LTspice 26.0.2 evaluated the original namespaced TI-family equations on the four-channel coax topology, with the native 910 kΩ difference-stage arms and R605 local 47 kΩ REF. From 300 Hz to 7 kHz, integrating the squared input-referred noise density for ADC1 gives **3.467 µVrms for the electronics**. Retaining the thermal noise of the four 50 Ω drivers, four 47 kΩ emulators and 47 kΩ REF gives **4.742 µVrms**. Both cases have identical source impedances. The corresponding integrated ADC-pin noise is 3.297 and 4.511 mVrms. These two numbers are analog pin noise before conversion.

Separate 5 V unity-gain model checks give 29.08 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz for TLV9002, compared with TI's 30 nV/√Hz typical for TLV900x, and 14.82 nV/√Hz at 2 kHz for TLV9052 against 15 nV/√Hz for TLV905x. The nominal modeled electronics meet the ≤6 µVrms objective. Actual ADC switching/reference noise, PCB pickup, operating corners and package coupling remain unmeasured. ## Crosstalk and midpoint stability

CH1 alone drives the single-aggressor test. In the three-aggressor test CH2–4 receive equal, in-phase sine voltages and CH1 stays quiet. REF and all inactive sources have their modeled return paths. The reported dB is the victim ADC response divided by the single driven channel's same-frequency direct gain, with equal source amplitude per aggressor. Where a source-equivalent error is quoted for 1 mVpp per aggressor it scales a small-signal AC result. The simulated symmetric quads have no intra-package crosstalk.

| Case | Worst single aggressor in 300 Hz–7 kHz | Worst three coherent aggressors | VMID voltage-injection unity crossover / phase margin estimate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Final native 910 kΩ schematic, 47 Ω isolation | −51.07 dB | −41.52 dB (8.39 µVpp) | 43.71 kHz / 88.7° |
| Earlier board-end 100 pF coax, 10 Ω isolation | −62.61 dB | −53.07 dB (2.22 µVpp) | 10.16 kHz / 71.7° |
| 47 Ω isolation, 100 pF board-end coax fixture | −51.05 dB | −41.51 dB (8.40 µVpp input-equivalent for 1 mVpp per aggressor) | 43.71 kHz / 88.7° |
| 47 Ω isolation, historical 20 pF lead sensitivity | −51.05 dB | −41.51 dB | 43.71 kHz / 88.7° |
| 4.7 Ω isolation, same 20 pF sensitivity | −64.34 dB | −54.80 dB (1.82 µVpp input-equivalent) | 6.73 kHz / 44.0° |

Retain the existing 47 Ω value for the first baseline. The 4.7 Ω result explains why a lower shared impedance helps channel isolation and why committing solely on crosstalk would cost stability margin. For the loop test, a 0 V DC / 1 V AC voltage source sits in the unity-feedback wire of U109B. The noninverting input and every channel retain their AC return. The script computes `T = −V(vmid_buf)/V(fb_sense)` and interpolates log frequency and unwrapped phase at 0 dB. The voltage-injection method assumes the follower output is low impedance relative to its feedback sense input. Package variation, cold startup, overload recovery and capacitor effective C/ESR/ESL still need verification. [Analog Devices’ capacitive-load simulation discussion](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/technical-articles/simulation-shows-how-real-op-amps-can-drive-capacitive-loads.html) · [Middlebrook’s measurement method](https://www.venableinstruments.com/venable-vault/measurement-of-loop-gain-in-feedback-systems)

## Electrical changes between eight and four populated channels

- Keep U101/U103/U105/U107 for the first four channels and U109 for shared REF/VMID and its two terminated spare units. Keep their local bypass capacitors, the 3 V rail bulk capacitor and the shared divider/bypass. U109C/D remain followers even when their outputs have no downstream loads.
- Omit whole quads U102/U104/U106/U108, their C1060/C1062/C1064/C1066, channel R1073–R1144 and C1029–C1056, and the retired ADC interface R405–408/C417–420. This removes all channel 5–8 bias, gain, filter and signal paths, including their loads on REF/VMID.
- The [four-coax schematic](../bench-four-coax/curalink-rev-a.kicad_sch) omits CH5–8 ESD quad U111, channel test pads TP113–TP124 and ADC test pads TP414–TP417. It retains protection on all used lines and REF. CH1–4 reach U401 physical pins 3, 4, 5 and 7. Pins 10, 12, 13 and 14 now carry CHG_STAT1, CHG_STAT2, RX_DC_PRESENT_MCU and CHG_ENABLED_MCU respectively. U401 pin 6 and associated bypass have moved to DVDD_3V0 in that variant. The AFE fixture terminates at the four ADC pads; injected digital noise and the changed converter rail/reference require separate tests. The PCB design must account for shield pads and local return vias.
- For the recommended bench implementation, use low-impedance attenuated drive through the four coax leads. Place the selectable 47 kΩ emulators at the receiving board. Leave a direct-drive option and document which jumpers/source settings apply to each test. Calibrate the attenuator in high-impedance mode: a generator amplitude specified into 50 Ω can approximately double when applied to the high-impedance input. A permanent 50 Ω shunt after the emulator would strongly attenuate the modeled electrode signal.

## Handoff and acceptance

The next laptop can use `spectra.csv`, `phasors.csv`, `operating-points.json`, `results.json` and the simulator logs without ADS. Each solved case also has a standalone native ADS deck. The earlier eight-channel control is under `nominal/` with its own deck and spectra. The four-channel driver starts from the already audited original [native netlist](../../wpt/ads/review-2026-11-22/afe/afe-dc-ac.ckt). It verifies the physically retained quads, the applied source vectors, all requested frequencies and the feedback injection. Rerun any four-channel case with the ADS Python interpreter and `check_afe.py <configuration>`; the case directory names identify its options.

The ≥10 MΩ whole-band input requirement remains a release gate. The sourced four-channel TLV baseline fails at the top of the band. A separate [MAX44280 candidate schematic and placement](../bench-four-coax-low-c/) has its own evidence. Hardware release still needs measured input impedance, an agreed interference budget, nominal and corner recovery, switched-ADC settling, channel order and BLE continuity, powered-off injection limits, measured four-channel noise, and a routed return-path/coupling audit. Test one active channel against three quiet channels, then three coherent aggressors against one quiet channel, and finally apply 100–300 µVpp spike waveforms. Capture board-input amplitude, ADC amplitude and reference voltages independently. For the 1 kHz nominal fixture a 200 µVpp differential sine gives about 194 mVpp at the ADC. Use representative source impedance, actual cables, and each connector orientation before accepting the board. The translated TI model has 140 internal DC-path corrections in the four-channel fixture and drops the noiseless-resistor setting. The separate LTspice noise analysis preserves the original model equations. ESD stress and assembled PCB coupling still require physical tests.
