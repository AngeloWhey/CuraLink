# MCU, RF and interface coupling review

Reviewed **2026-10-02, America/New_York**. Status: schematic review and proposed layout constraints. The reviewed sources are the canonical MCU, interface, AFE and power component files; their hashes and exact MCU/interface nets are saved in [the evidence manifest](mcu-rf/source-manifest.json) and [net snapshot](mcu-rf/reviewed-net-snapshot.json). No routed `.kicad_pcb` or `.PcbDoc` was present in this workspace at review time. This report therefore identifies sensitive regions and tests; it does not establish existing trace clearances, fabricated impedances or measured crosstalk.

## Findings that should drive placement

1. **The RF feed has two different line requirements.** U401 pin 37 → U402 A1 is the `MCU_RF` segment, specified by the selected filter manufacturer as **63 Ω characteristic impedance**. U402 A3 → the antenna/coax selection is the `RF_50OHM` segment, specified as **50 Ω**. These are transmission-line requirements, not instructions to place 63 Ω or 50 Ω resistors. ST requires geometry to be recalculated for the actual stackup. Do not copy reference widths to another dielectric height, copper thickness or coplanar clearance. [MLPF-WB-04D3 DS14446 Rev 6 §3.1, Figs. 13–14](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/mlpf-wb-04d3.pdf)

2. **AE401 needs an antenna region, not merely its component footprint.** Its isolated mechanical terminal, ground clearance, surrounding board ground, final battery, coil and enclosure all matter. The current tee has adjustment positions, but its **C423 = 1 pF, L401 = 2.7 nH, L402 = 3.9 nH** are evaluation-board starting values. They do not prove a match on CuraLink's board. Johanson explicitly makes the match dependent on PCB geometry/material; ST also warns against a battery beneath the antenna. [Johanson Rev 4.0 pp. 2–3](https://www.johansontechnology.com/docs/3827/Antenna-2450AT18A0100001E-Rev4.0.pdf), [ST AN5948 Rev 4 §7.8](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an5948-how-to-develop-rf-hardware-using-stm32wba-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf)

3. **ADC accuracy depends on the local ground and reference, as well as the eight signal wires.** The selected package uses VDDA (`AVDD_3V0`, pin 6) as its ADC reference; `VMID_RAW` is the AFE bias and is not an ADC reference pin. R401–R408/C413–C420 are independent **150 Ω / 470 pF** charge-reservoir networks. Their settling simulations do not include actual board coupling, trace capacitance, probe loading or reference/ground disturbance. [STM32WBA52CG DS14127 Rev 10 Fig. 14 and ADC characteristics](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32wba52cg.pdf), [ST AN2834 Rev 10 §§3.2.11, 4.2.11–4.2.14](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an2834-how-to-optimize-the-adc-accuracy-in-the-stm32-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf)

4. **Interleaved FFC grounds help, but do not establish channel isolation.** J301 alternates each signal with GND, including the reference. Cable and board parasitics still load the nominal 47 kΩ stimulus sources. A waveform intentionally split into all eight channels cannot demonstrate crosstalk: a separate test must drive one channel and hold the other inputs quiet through representative source impedances.

| MCU regulator outputs: **VCAP pin 44/C408 4.7 µF/TP408**; **VDDHPA pin 38/C409 470 nF/TP409** | Added inductance or unintended loading on internal regulator outputs; test stubs. | Keep the capacitors adjacent to their pins and their loops compact. These are output-decoupling nets, not 3 V supply inputs or spare rails. Do not use them to power other circuitry. Use small inspection pads, not long branches. |
| Digital/radio supply: VDD pins **11/22/35/43**, VDDRF **39**, VDDRFPA **42**; C401–C405/C410/C411; U401 EP **49** | Radio/digital current enters the shared supply and ground; constricted returns can modulate the ADC reference. | Give the local bypass capacitors short pin-to-capacitor-to-ground loops. | HSE: **Y401**, `HSE_IN` pin **41**, `HSE_OUT` pin **40** | Clock pickup/radiation and parasitic capacitance affect oscillator startup/frequency, including the radio clock. | Place Y401 close to these pins and keep traces short, on one layer where practical, without unrelated routes between them. | LSE: **Y402**, pins **17/16**, `LSE_IN`/`LSE_OUT`; C421/C422 **4.7 pF** | High-impedance oscillator nodes are sensitive to leakage, switching pickup and stray capacitance. | Keep the crystal/capacitor loops compact and away from debug, radio and high-current routes. | RF matching/filter: **U401.37 → U402.A1**, U402 ground **A2/B1/B2/B3**, U402.A3 → `RF_50OHM` | Traces, pads, return discontinuities and filter grounding become part of the RF circuit. | Keep the path short; use the component-specific stackup and land-pattern guidance. Populate one selection at a time. J404 contact is project pad 1 and shell pads 2/3; verify the physical footprint against the maker drawing. |
| Antenna tee and radiator: **C423 series**, **L401 shunt**, **L402 series**, **AE401**; `ANT_T_IN`, `ANT_T_MID`, `ANT_FEED` | Matching-pad parasitics; magnetic coupling between inductors; nearby metal and ground alter radiation/match. | Put the tee at the antenna feed. Give the shunt inductor a short ground return; assess relative inductor orientation. Implement the antenna drawing's mounting/clearance geometry and the project's all-layer antenna keepout, with only the required feed/isolated anchor copper. Keep battery and WPT coil metal out of this region. |
| Debug/UART: **J401/J402**, R411–R415 **47 Ω**, SWDIO/SWCLK/SWO/UART; `ACQ_MARKER`, `GPIO_TEST` | Fast edges can couple despite a low bit rate; external cables change return currents and may back-power a disabled target. | Route these away from high-impedance input/reference and oscillator nodes, over an uninterrupted return. Place any source-damping resistor with the actual driver/edge direction in mind. J401/J402 rail pads are voltage sense only; external equipment must not drive an unpowered target. The 47 Ω resistors provide no isolation. |
| FFC: **J301**, `FFC_CH1`…`FFC_CH8`, `FFC_REF`, GND pins and mounting pads | Adjacent-conductor capacitance, common cable return, input loading and mating reversal. | Place the connector on the input side of the AFE; keep these paths short and away from antenna, clocks and WPT loops. ## Return-path and stackup choice

Use the common `GND` as an uninterrupted reference for signal returns, with physical separation of sensitive input/reference regions and radio/digital/power current loops. Avoid a plane slot beneath a signal, a shared narrow ground neck between regions, or a long return caused by routing a signal across an arbitrary analog/digital split. Separate analog/digital supply labels and separate LDOs U512/U513 do not eliminate their common upstream rail or common-ground impedance. This is a proposed CuraLink implementation rule; AN2834's generic star/split examples are not evidence that cutting this board's ground plane improves it.

Select the actual fabrication stackup before defining RF widths and coplanar clearances. AN5948's general checklist and the MLPF-specific reference must be reconciled: the filter datasheet/reference has its own stack geometry. Follow the selected component's detailed layout, or recalculate and document the adapted RF cross-section. The antenna clearance is a deliberate local exception to continuous copper, so unrelated signal routes must not cross that clearance. [ST AN6335 Rev 1 §6/Fig. 13](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an6335-mlpfwb-and-mlpfnrg-pcb-design-guidelines-and-reference-hardware-stmicroelectronics.pdf)

No fixed trace-to-trace spacing can be justified without layer positions, copper geometry, parallel-run length, dielectric properties, driver edge rate and permitted error. In particular, “3W spacing” is not a quantified microvolt crosstalk guarantee. Board dimensions/ground extent and antenna clearance must be checked together before the outline is frozen.

## FFC and useful verification

The canonical J301 map is **1 CH1, 2 GND, 3 CH2, 4 GND, …, 15 CH8, 16 GND, 17 REF, 18 GND**; mounting pads MP1/MP2 are GND. `REF` is the analog reference input and must not be repurposed as a ground or power conductor. The proposed 50 mm cable is not an independently shielded set of eight pairs.

Before PCB release, inspect the actual layer views for the table's checks, record the RF cross-sections/stackup, check copper in the antenna region on every layer, and check battery/coil/enclosure positions. CAD connectivity/DRC alone cannot validate those couplings. A routed-board review also needs component coordinates, plane fills, vias and physical cable routing.

On the bench, drive **one** channel through its representative source resistance, hold the other seven quiet through representative impedances, and keep REF fixed. Measure victim/aggressor amplitude and phase across the acquisition band; report the ratio in dB and the input-referred victim voltage using the measured channel gain. Repeat with different driven channels and with BLE disabled/active/TX, debug attached/detached, and test probes attached/removed. This separates channel coupling from common-reference/supply pickup and probe loading. Repeat any antenna/range comparison with the final battery, coil and enclosure positions; a coax-selected conducted test does not establish antenna efficiency.

For WPT interference, the charge/run interlock does not prevent magnetic pickup from an externally energized 13.56 MHz coil. Keep the coil/rectifier AC-current loop away from the FFC and AFE; evaluate induced/common-mode pickup with a bounded, safe bench setup. Do not extrapolate the audio-band AFE macromodel into a 13.56 MHz or 2.4 GHz rejection claim: high-frequency device response, parasitic coupling and demodulation are absent from that model. No PCB coupling simulation or physical RF/EM measurement has been completed in this review.
