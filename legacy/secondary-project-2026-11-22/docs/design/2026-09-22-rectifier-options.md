# CuraLink 13.56 MHz rectifier topology comparison

**2026-09-22 · Candidate study, no rectifier selected.** This follows the user-requested choice format: criteria first; each option described in one or two sentences with parallel pros/cons; then a recommendation and conditions for revisiting it. The [handbook](../engineering-handbook.md) leaves the exact rectifier open. Under [DEC-WPT-003's revised simulation method](2026-09-22-wpt-simulation-method.md), the options will first be compared in ADS with the published measured L/R/k model for the 13.56 MHz Luo et al. 40 mm TX / 10 mm RX planar pair at 10 mm nominal separation. No CuraLink RX power or voltage result exists yet, so the recommendation remains conditional.

## Basis for choosing

1. **Hard charger window:** The rectifier plus reservoir and protection must deliver enough voltage/current at the worst intended coil gap/alignment and must not exceed downstream ratings at best coupling or with a full/absent battery. The nominal ~5 V WPT_RAW target is not a guaranteed supply.
2. **Primary FOCs:** Rank **delivered DC power at useful voltage** and RF-to-DC efficiency at a stated TX-coil input power/port plane. Coil Q, k and S21 remain explanatory diagnostics. Charging time/current and the exact cell are still needed to turn this into a numeric minimum DC-power requirement.
3. **Two-terminal coil compatibility:** The Luo et al. RX reference is modeled as a conventional two-terminal spiral, so the primary candidates are the bridge and doubler. Do not introduce a center tap without redoing the EM and matching design.
4. **13.56 MHz behavior:** At the actual RF current, compare forward drop, junction capacitance, reverse leakage, reverse-voltage/surge rating, package parasitics and thermal dissipation. Rectifier loading changes the resonance and matching, so retune each candidate fairly using the DEC-WPT-003 measured coupled-inductor baseline or a replacement `.s2p` when HFSS is required. [Nexperia Schottky application note](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/application-note/AN90056.pdf), [Keysight rectifier co-simulation example](https://www.keysight.com/in/en/assets/7018-05801/case-studies/5992-2395.pdf).
5. **Protection and practical build:** Consider startup at weak coupling, no-load overvoltage, capacitor inrush, RX-side WPT-presence sensing before a disconnect, part count, footprint and first-board test access. The AFE is off during charging, so neural-recording noise is not a reason by itself to prefer one rectifier; charger input ripple and WPT control still matter.

## Options

| Type | One- or two-sentence description | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast silicon p–n diode bridge | Four diodes form a full-wave bridge across a two-terminal RX coil; two conduct on each half cycle. | Simple, no startup controller, familiar full-wave output, often lower reverse leakage than Schottky. | Higher forward loss at low received voltage and charge/recovery effects at RF; a generic mains bridge is inappropriate at 13.56 MHz. |
| **Low-capacitance Schottky full-wave bridge** | Four RF-suitable Schottky diodes provide full-wave rectification from a two-terminal coil; two conduct per half cycle. | Passive startup, both half cycles used, smaller forward drop than silicon, simple to model and debug, no coil tap. | Two forward drops and conduction pulses cost power; junction capacitance, temperature-dependent leakage, peak-current/reverse-voltage rating and no-load voltage must be checked. |
| Single-diode Schottky half-wave | One diode charges a reservoir on one half cycle of the RX waveform. | Lowest part count and one forward drop, useful as a quick characterization reference. | Does not use the other half cycle, increases ripple/reservoir demand and can load the resonant coil asymmetrically; unlikely to maximize useful charge power. |
| Two-diode capacitor voltage doubler | Diodes and capacitors combine alternating peaks to raise DC voltage above a simple bridge's output for a weak RX voltage. | Can reach charger input voltage when a bridge falls short; one-diode-path advantage may help at low input power. | Voltage gain does **not** create extra power; output impedance/ripple and peak component voltage can be worse at charge current, especially no load. Re-evaluate efficiency after matching and real load. [Analog Devices doubler explanation](https://wiki.analog.com/university/courses/electronics/text/chapter-6). |
| Center-tapped two-diode full-wave | If the RX coil supplies a genuine center tap, one diode rectifies each half-cycle against that tap. | Full-wave output with one conducting diode path. | Requires a three-terminal coil and new EM/impedance analysis; each half-winding and diode reverse stress change. It is not applicable to an already-fixed two-terminal coil. [Analog Devices rectifier tutorial](https://wiki.analog.com/university/courses/electronics/text/chapter-6). |
| MOSFET synchronous/active full bridge | Controlled MOSFETs replace rectifier diodes so conduction loss can fall when current is high enough. | Potentially higher efficiency once drive/control overhead is amortized; integrated receivers can also add control and protection. | Gate timing, cold start, reverse current, quiescent power and 13.56 MHz switching/verification effort are substantial. Published active 13.56 MHz rectifiers are often custom ICs rather than drop-in discrete solutions. [Primary 13.56 MHz rectifier research](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8649958/). |
| Integrated wireless/NFC receiver | An IC may combine rectification with regulation, protection and sometimes protocol functions. | Compact controlled system if its frequency, coil, power and protocol exactly match. | Do not substitute a Qi receiver for this 13.56 MHz study: an example Qi device operates in the 110–205 kHz WPC range. An example 13.56 MHz NFC harvester is specified up to 30 mW, which may be insufficient for a desired cell-charge rate. [TI Qi receiver datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq51013b.pdf), [NXP NTAG 5 fact sheet](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/fact-sheet/NTAG5SWITCHFS.pdf). |

## Working recommendation and decision test

Use a **low-capacitance Schottky full-wave bridge** as the Rev A *simulation and bench baseline* for a two-terminal RX coil. It balances passive startup, full-wave energy use, small/inspectable implementation and low enough conceptual complexity to isolate coil/tuning failures. This is **not** a final diode or topology selection: at 13.56 MHz, a low DC forward voltage alone is insufficient; choose a model/part against capacitance, leakage, current pulses and no-load reverse voltage. A Schottky bridge does **not** regulate WPT_RAW or protect the charger by itself.

The paper's approximately 70% result is a **matched RF coil-link measurement**, obtained from `S21 = -1.55 dB` with 50 Ω VNA source/load ports and an L-network at 10 mm. It does not include a rectifier, reservoir, regulator or charger and therefore is not an RF-to-DC or battery-charging efficiency. The paper reports measured coil values at 13.56 MHz of approximately `L_TX = 2.49 µH`, `R_TX = 1.80 Ω`, `Q_TX = 118`, `L_RX = 0.330 µH`, `R_RX = 0.399 Ω`, `Q_RX = 70.4`, `M = 60.0 nH` and `k = 0.0662`; DEC-WPT-003 selects these as the Rev A ADS starting model, and D-019 accepts that CuraLink cannot independently verify them with a VNA. [Luo et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10302922/).

In ADS, compare the bridge against a **two-diode doubler** with the same measured L/R/k coil baseline, TX accepted RF input power, assumed coupling case and several useful resistor loads. Retune matching separately for each. An illustrative 200 Ω load corresponds to 5 V/25 mA, **not** a selected charge current; include lighter and heavier loads and no load. Report `V_DC`, `P_DC=V_DC²/R`, `η_RF→DC`, input reflection/accepted power, diode peak current/reverse voltage/loss, reservoir ripple, startup and full-battery/no-load WPT_RAW. Later replace the resistor with the selected charger's input behavior.

**Keep the Schottky bridge** if it meets charger minimum voltage and required delivered DC power at worst intended coupling, has manageable losses/temperature and bounded no-load voltage with separate OVP/regulation. **Revisit a doubler** if voltage is short but available RF power is adequate; **revisit synchronous rectification** only if measured/modelled diode loss prevents the power/efficiency goal enough to justify control complexity. A center-tapped option is conditional on the actual coil. No performance claim or accepted design decision follows before the coil and load results are available.
