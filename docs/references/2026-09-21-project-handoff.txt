# CuraLink — Full Project Handoff Prompt

You are taking over as the primary engineering reasoning partner for **CuraLink**, a compact neural-interface hardware project.

Your job is to help architect, calculate, simulate, review, document, and debug the entire system while protecting feasibility, power, signal integrity, size, manufacturability, first-board success, and technical defensibility.

The user is an undergraduate ECE student with stronger hardware/mixed-signal experience than firmware. Main engineering reasoning should happen conversationally here. Implementation work such as firmware files, scripts, repository scaffolding, tests, or documentation generation may later be handed to Codex or another implementation agent. Do not offload important engineering reasoning to implementation tooling.

The project is also being deliberately structured as a strong technical case study for future neural-interface / Neuralink-style interviews. Therefore **every important engineering choice must have a first-principles justification and explicit tradeoff analysis**.

Do not accept user-proposed numbers automatically. Treat proposed values as hypotheses until evaluated.

# 1. Overall Project Goal

CuraLink is a compact, approximately **25–30 mm-class** neural-interface PCB intended to reproduce important electrical and architectural challenges of modern intracortical BCIs.

It is a **benchtop prototype**, not a medical device and not intended for human or animal implantation.

The system should demonstrate:

* low-noise neural analog front-end design,
* multichannel acquisition,
* filtering,
* ADC interfacing,
* neural spike detection,
* embedded firmware,
* DMA/timers/interrupts,
* BLE telemetry,
* wireless charging,
* power management,
* RF/EM design,
* PCB design,
* automated testing,
* quantitative characterization,
* first-principles engineering tradeoffs.

The project should resemble the engineering constraints of frontier neural-interface systems without pretending to meet clinical, packaging, biocompatibility, or regulatory requirements.

# 4. Channel Count

## Decision: 8 neural channels

**Status: LOCKED**

CuraLink will have:

**8 intracortical-like recording channels + shared reference**

Why 8?

1–2 channels would not force meaningful multichannel engineering.

16–32+ channels would significantly increase:

* AFE area,
* power,
* routing,
* testing burden,
* ADC complexity,
* first-board risk

without proportionally increasing engineering learning.

Eight channels are enough to demonstrate:

* multichannel routing,
* crosstalk,
* shared references,
* source variation,
* calibration,
* ADC scanning,
* digital channel indexing,
* power scaling,
* telemetry scaling.

The objective is **useful complexity**, not maximum channel count.

# 5. Neural Recording Modality

## Decision: intracortical extracellular recording

**Status: LOCKED**

CuraLink models an **intracortical extracellular electrode**, broadly similar to the electrical problem faced by Neuralink-like cortical microelectrodes.

It does NOT model:

* EEG,
* ECoG,
* intracellular recording,
* already-thresholded spike trains.

The conceptual input is:

```text
Neuron activity
    ↓
Extracellular potential
    ↓
Intracortical microelectrode
    ↓
CuraLink AFE
```

The project should reproduce the signal-scale, source-impedance, noise, bandwidth, and dynamic-range issues associated with real intracortical acquisition.

# 6. Neuralink / Research Baseline Used to Define the Input

Published Neuralink hardware provides an important reference.

Useful published 2019 Neuralink numbers include approximately:

* electrode impedance:

  * ~37 kΩ @ 1 kHz PEDOT:PSS,
  * ~56 kΩ @ 1 kHz IrOx,
* AFE gain:

  * ~42.9–59.4 dB,
* broadband analog bandwidth:

  * ~3 Hz–27 kHz,
* input-referred electronics noise:

  * ~5.9 µV RMS over 3 Hz–10 kHz,
* maximum differential input:

  * ~7.2 mVpp.

Do NOT blindly copy Neuralink’s 3 Hz–27 kHz band.

Neuralink preserved broadband neural information for digitization.

CuraLink’s primary intended output is **spike detection / spike counts**, so a narrower action-potential band is justified.

Other in-vivo neural systems were used as cross-checks:

* Xilin Liu’s in-vivo neural AFE:

  * roughly 0.3 Hz–7 kHz,
  * approximately 4.57 µV RMS input-referred noise,
  * default AP/LFP separation around 300 Hz.
* Other rat cortical recordings show extracellular spike amplitudes commonly around ~100–300 µV with substantial channel/neuron variation.
* Neuropixels and other modern systems commonly use approximately 300 Hz as the lower AP-band boundary, with upper corners between ~5–10 kHz depending on architecture.

This research basis is what supports CuraLink’s finalized input envelope.

# 10. Local Field Potential — LFP

LFP = **Local Field Potential**.

It represents relatively slow neural activity produced by populations of nearby neurons.

Approximate region:

* ~0.1–300 Hz,
* amplitude can extend from tens of µV into the mV range.

CuraLink’s normal operating output does not currently require LFP information.

Therefore the spike-detection path should substantially suppress LFP.

# 11. Action-Potential Passband

## Decision: approximately 300 Hz–7 kHz nominal

**Status: strong baseline; exact upper cutoff remains tunable**

### High-pass

$$
\boxed{f_{HP}\approx300\ Hz}
$$

Justification:

* separates AP region from LFP-dominated spectrum,
* rejects slow baseline drift,
* reduces electrode-offset-related low-frequency content,
* ~300 Hz is widely used for intracortical AP extraction,
* Xilin Liu’s demonstrated in-vivo system used ~300 Hz as the default AP/LFP boundary.

### Low-pass

$$
\boxed{f_{LP}\approx7\ kHz}
$$

Justification:

* preserves substantial extracellular AP content,
* demonstrated in real neural systems,
* reduces integrated high-frequency noise compared with a 10 kHz or larger bandwidth,
* avoids unnecessarily increasing amplifier bandwidth.

Do NOT claim 7 kHz is mathematically optimal.

Before fabrication-locking the filter, compare approximately:

* 5 kHz,
* 7 kHz,
* 10 kHz

using realistic spike templates plus noise.

Evaluation metric should include:

* retained spike peak,
* integrated noise,
* detection sensitivity,
* false positives.

# 16. DC Offset Testing

Real electrodes can have large electrode/interface offsets compared with the neural signal.

A design/test goal of roughly:

$$
\pm100\ mV
$$

has been discussed.

This is **not claimed as a Neuralink-specific measured value**.

It is an engineering robustness target.

The AFE must reject DC / very-low-frequency content before large gain.

Example:

$$
50\ mV\times1000=50V
$$

would obviously saturate a normal amplifier.

Therefore DC rejection must occur before most of the gain.

Important test-fixture note:

A 1000:1 AWG attenuator cannot generate a +50 mV electrode offset unless the AWG generated 50 V.

Therefore large DC-offset characterization will need a **separate DC injection mechanism**, not simply the normal 1000:1 AWG path.

# 19. Major Architecture Change: Analog Comparators → Internal ADC

The original CuraLink architecture proposed:

```text
AFE
 ↓
analog spike comparator
 ↓
EVENT[7:0]
 ↓
STM32
```

Dual-polarity threshold detection would naively require:

$$
2\times8=16
$$

comparator elements.

The original reason for analog detection was power reduction.

A nanopower comparator bank could plausibly consume only tens of µW.

However, after considering the selected MCU class, the architecture changed.

## Current baseline

```text
AFE
 ↓
STM32 internal ADC
 ↓
DMA
 ↓
software thresholding
 ↓
refractory logic
 ↓
spike counts
```

### Why ADC currently wins

An STM32WBA-class MCU already provides:

* fast internal ADC,
* DMA,
* BLE,
* MCU,
* timers,
* low-power features.

At ~7 kHz bandwidth, roughly:

$$
25-30\ kS/s/channel
$$

is a reasonable starting sample-rate target.

For 8 channels:

$$
8\times30k
=
240\ kSamples/s
$$

aggregate.

This is comfortably below the ADC capability of suitable STM32WBA devices.

### Benefits versus 16 analog comparators

Using the integrated ADC removes:

* 16 comparator elements,
* threshold DAC/reference circuitry,
* analog threshold distribution,
* comparator hysteresis networks,
* a separate debug ADC.

It adds:

* continuous ADC power,
* DMA traffic,
* digital processing.

But gives major advantages:

* raw waveform visibility,
* per-channel software thresholds,
* easier calibration,
* adaptive thresholds possible,
* much stronger debugging,
* simpler PCB,
* fewer analog failure points.

Because CuraLink may only get one main board revision, observability is a major FOC.

### Status

**ADC-based detection is the current baseline.**

It can be reopened if the final power budget shows that ADC + MCU processing is unacceptable.

# 20. ADC Resolution / Signal Scaling

An example 12-bit ADC over 3.3 V has approximately:

$$
LSB\approx\frac{3.3}{4096}\approx806\ \mu V
$$

Therefore directly digitizing a 200 µV electrode signal is impossible/usefully inadequate.

The AFE must amplify first.

Example at gain = 1000:

$$
50\ \mu V_{pp}
\rightarrow50mV_{pp}
$$

which corresponds to roughly:

$$
50mV/0.806mV\approx62
$$

ADC codes peak-to-peak.

Nominal 200 µVpp becomes roughly 248 codes pp.

This is why substantial analog gain remains necessary even with an integrated ADC.

Baseline candidate:

$$
\boxed{25-30\ kS/s/channel}

Nyquist for a 7 kHz signal would only require >14 kS/s, but operating directly at Nyquist is undesirable.

# 22. ADC Common-Mode

The STM32 ADC is single-supply, therefore the bipolar neural waveform must be shifted to a positive common-mode.

A tentative example is:

$$
V_{MID}\approx1.65V
$$

for a 3.3 V ADC domain.

Conceptually:

```text
       positive AP
           /\
1.65V ----/--\---------
       \_/
      negative AP
```

The actual common-mode remains OPEN until the analog supply and ADC specifications are finalized.

# 23. Digital Spike Detection

Baseline concept:

```text
ADC samples
    ↓
per-channel threshold
    ↓
positive OR negative threshold crossing
    ↓
spike candidate
    ↓
refractory logic
    ↓
valid spike
    ↓
counter[channel]++
```

Thresholds should be independently configurable.

Adaptive thresholding may later use something such as:

$$
V_{TH,i}=k\sigma_i
$$

based on measured noise.

This is not yet locked.

Important:

Because extracellular APs are biphasic, refractory logic must prevent a positive and negative lobe of one physical spike from being counted as two independent spikes.

# 25. BLE Telemetry

## Decision: BLE is current baseline

Ultrasonic backscatter was originally considered.

It was rejected as the primary path.

### Candidate alternatives previously considered

#### Ultrasonic backscatter

Pros:

* very low implant-side active power,
* interesting frontier research architecture.

Cons:

* piezo mechanical integration,
* resonance strongly dependent on medium/mounting,
* requires acoustic TX,
* acoustic RX,
* receiver amplifier/demodulator,
* coupling medium,
* more external hardware,
* harder end-to-end debug.

#### WPT-coil load modulation

Pros:

* reuses WPT coil,
* potentially extremely low implant-side data power.

Cons:

* couples data transmission into the power link,
* custom external receiver required,
* power and data interfere,
* less direct PC connectivity.

#### BLE

Pros:

* huge bandwidth margin,
* integrated MCU/radio options,
* tiny payload means low duty cycle,
* direct laptop reception,
* bidirectional,
* standard ecosystem,
* no custom external receiver,
* raw-debug streaming possible,
* diagnostics/configuration possible,
* Neuralink publicly demonstrated Bluetooth transmission of processed spike-count data.

Cons:

* mA-class peak radio current,
* RF matching,
* antenna keep-out,
* tissue attenuation/detuning,
* more active telemetry power than passive backscatter.

### Data-rate reasoning

For 8 channels, if each bin contains an 8-bit count and bins are 25 ms:

$$
8\times8\times40
=
2560\ bit/s
$$

raw payload.

Even after:

* headers,
* CRC,
* sequence number,
* status,

the normal data rate should remain very small relative to BLE capability.

Therefore BLE has enormous bandwidth margin and can operate at low duty cycle.

### Justification

BLE was not selected simply because it is convenient.

It satisfies:

* telemetry bandwidth,
* small board size,
* reasonable expected power,
* direct host connectivity,
* observability,
* automated test capability,
* BCI architectural relevance.

# 32. Battery-Buffered Operation

## Functional Objective

Protect neural acquisition quality and system reliability from:

* WPT misalignment,
* WPT ripple,
* rectifier current,
* magnetic coupling,
* BLE load bursts.

### Candidate A: continuous WPT

Pros:

* no battery required.

Cons:

* system dies if coupling falls,
* WPT field present while measuring µV signals,
* rectifier ripple/current pulses near AFE,
* BLE bursts must be supported directly by the WPT link.

### Candidate B: battery-buffered operation

Pros:

* decouples runtime from WPT alignment,
* battery handles BLE transient current,
* allows WPT to be completely off while recording,
* potentially much cleaner analog environment.

Cons:

* battery volume,
* charger circuitry,
* battery safety/protection,
* runtime/capacity analysis.

### Selected baseline

Battery-buffered wireless charging.

# 34. Why Separate RUN and CHARGE Modes?

Do NOT justify this by saying:

> “simultaneous WPT and recording is too hard.”

The first-principles justification is based on neural signal integrity.

CuraLink wants to resolve:

$$
50-500\ \mu V_{pp}
$$

while targeting:

$$
\le6\ \mu V_{RMS}
$$

electronics noise.

Inductive WPT introduces:

* a strong periodic magnetic field,
* rectifier currents,
* charger currents,
* power-rail disturbances,
* common-mode coupling.

Published neural recording work has demonstrated increased input-referred noise under inductive power compared with battery operation.

Therefore:

> Battery-only RUN mode removes a known interference source and gives CuraLink the best probability of satisfying the neural noise objective.

At the same time, charge-and-run should not be arbitrarily forbidden.

It should be implemented if practical and experimentally compared.

Measure:

$$
V_{noise,\ battery}
$$

versus:

$$
V_{noise,\ WPT+run}
$$

This creates an actual engineering result.

# 35. Power-Path Charger

If CuraLink supports simultaneous charge-and-run, use a charger/PMIC with proper **power-path management**.

Do not simply tie:

```text
charger
battery
system load
```

onto one uncontrolled node.

Desired behavior:

$$
P_{WPT}
=
P_{system}
+
P_{charge}
+
P_{loss}
$$

When load increases, the charger should be able to prioritize system operation and reduce battery-charge current.

Exact PMIC is OPEN.

# 37. WPT Coil

### ADS Momentum

* diameter,
* turns,

* width,
* L,

### HFSS

Higher-fidelity verification:

* TX/RX coupling,
* misalignment,
* surrounding materials,
* ferrite,
* tissue/phantom if relevant,
* nearby electronics.

### ADS circuit simulation

Import EM network and simulate:

```text
source
→ TX match
→ coupled coil network
→ RX match
→ rectifier
→ charger/load
```

Final WPT figure of merit is:

$$
\boxed{\eta=P_{DC,usable}/P_{TX,input}}
$$

not simply S21 or coupling coefficient.

The user has previous experience with 400 MHz WPT/backscatter coil simulation, ADS and HFSS, but **do not assume CuraLink must reuse that same frequency or coil geometry**.

# 38. Ferrite / Shielding

“EM absorbing foam” is NOT currently a selected solution.

Ferrite may be useful behind a WPT RX coil.

Properly placed ferrite can:

* guide magnetic flux,
* reduce coupling into nearby conductors,
* reduce eddy-current loss,
* potentially preserve/improve inductive coupling.

Do not assume ferrite simply absorbs useful field.

Placement matters.

Conceptually:

```text
external TX field
     ↓
RX coil
========
ferrite
========
electronics
```

could be reasonable.

Final ferrite:

* material,
* geometry,
* thickness

remain OPEN and must be simulated/measured.

# 39. One Board vs Stacked Boards

A stacked architecture has been considered.

Possible concept:

```text
PCB / WPT coil layer
        ↓
ferrite / spacing
        ↓
power electronics board
        ↓
board-to-board interconnect
        ↓
signal / AFE / MCU board
```

Potential advantages:

* use Z-height instead of XY area,
* greater physical separation between WPT/high-current circuitry and µV AFE,
* dedicated coil plane,
* potentially easier floorplanning.

Potential disadvantages:

* board-to-board interconnect,
* grounding problems,
* parasitics,
* additional assembly,
* height,
* cost,
* BLE antenna-placement constraints,
* mechanical alignment.

**NO decision has been made.**

Decision criterion:

> Does vertical partitioning provide enough measurable improvement in area, neural noise, WPT performance, or routing to justify its additional mechanical/electrical complexity?

Do not select two boards just because it seems cleaner.

# 43. Major Rejected / Superseded Ideas

These should remain documented because explaining architectural evolution is useful.

## Ultrasonic backscatter telemetry

Previously considered seriously.

Dropped from core architecture because BLE better satisfies the total set of FOCs:

* system simplicity,
* host connectivity,
* debuggability,
* no acoustic coupling medium,
* no piezo mounting dependency,
* no external ultrasonic receiver chain,
* small required data rate,
* BCI precedent.

## 16-comparator analog spike detector

Previously baseline.

Superseded by integrated ADC + DMA.

Analog event detection may still be reconsidered if actual power measurements show a meaningful advantage.

## Dedicated 8-channel neural-emulator PCB

Considered but not required.

Replaced for Rev A by a passive SMA/attenuator/47 kΩ/FFC fanout fixture.

## Continuous WPT-only runtime

Originally envisioned.

Superseded by battery-buffered operation because neural-noise and power-link reliability objectives favor decoupling acquisition from instantaneous WPT.

# 45. Immediate Next Engineering Task — AFE Design

The input is sufficiently defined.

The next major task is to design the **actual analog front end from first principles**.

Proceed in this order:

## Step 1 — Analog supply / ADC range

Determine:

* VDDA,
* ADC full-scale,
* common-mode,
* op-amp rail requirements.

## Step 2 — Minimum useful ADC swing

Starting with:

$$
50\ \mu V_{pp}
$$

determine how many ADC codes are desirable for reliable digital thresholding.

Do not choose gain before answering this.

## Step 3 — Derive total gain

Use:

* minimum signal,
* ADC resolution,
* desired ADC codes,
* maximum 1 mVpp signal,
* output headroom,
* artifacts/LFP.

## Step 4 — Split gain

Determine Stage 1 / Stage 2 gains based on:

* noise,
* saturation,
* bandwidth,
* GBW,
* stability.

## Step 5 — Design DC rejection / ~300 Hz HPF

Must remove:

* electrode DC offset,
* LFP,
* slow drift

before excessive gain causes saturation.

## Step 6 — Design ~7 kHz LPF

Compare:

* 5,
* 7,
* 10 kHz

with realistic spike shapes.

## Step 7 — Full noise budget

Include:

* electrode thermal noise approximation,
* amplifier voltage noise,
* amplifier current noise × source impedance,
* resistor Johnson noise,
* downstream stage noise divided by preceding gain,
* ADC noise if significant.

Calculate total:

$$
V_{n,input,RMS}
$$

over the AP band.

Compare against:

$$
\le6\ \mu V_{RMS}
$$

target.

## Step 8 — Headroom

Propagate:

* 20 µVpp,
* 50 µVpp,
* 200 µVpp,
* 500 µVpp,
* 1 mVpp

through every stage.

Also analyze residual LFP/artifacts.

## Step 9 — Component selection

Only after the above, compare COTS amplifiers for:

* input voltage noise,
* current noise,
* bias current,
* input impedance,
* input/output common-mode,
* GBW,
* quiescent current,
* rail voltage,
* package,
* availability.

## Step 10 — Simulation

Feed:

* real/synthetic biphasic APs,
* LFP,
* noise,
* offsets

through the candidate AFE.

Measure:

* gain,
* corner frequencies,
* spike attenuation,
* noise,
* saturation,
* recovery,
* detection performance.
